## Description
The prosecutor never once asks Meursault why he pulled the trigger. He asks why he didn't cry at his mother's funeral — and the jury convicts on that.

In this deep analysis of "The Stranger" by Albert Camus, we unpack the real argument hiding inside the most misread novel in modern literature. This isn't a book about a man who feels nothing — it's about a society that destroys anyone who refuses to perform. We trace every key moment: the funeral no one actually watches, Marie's impossible question, Raymond's trap, the sun on the beach, the trial that puts grief on the stand, and the explosive chaplain scene that changes everything.
