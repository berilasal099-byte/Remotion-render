A boy, a ring, and a choice that cannot be undone. This is a summary and analysis of The Magician's Nephew by C.S. Lewis.

In London in the summer of 1900, Digory Kirke is stuck in a dusty house with his dying mother upstairs. His Uncle Andrew, a vain amateur magician, uses yellow and green magic rings to send children into other worlds. Digory and Polly Plummer end up in the dead city of Charn, where Jadis, its last queen, wakes when a bell is struck. The rings carry Jadis into London, and then the children are pulled into a newborn Narnia.

This video follows the book's central question: what a person does with power they cannot undo. We look at why Uncle Andrew never takes the physical risk he expects others to take, how Digory's choice turns the power of the rings into a responsibility, and why the apple he brings back grows into the tree that shelters Narnia.
